A simple fabric profile

Roller shades

A single panel of fabric rolls up at the top of the window. Roller shades give you a clean look, with the fabric choice determining how much light comes through and how much privacy you have.

Compare: light-filtering and room-darkening fabrics, color, texture, and how the shade mounts at your window.

More about roller shades

Glare and outward views

Solar shades

Solar shades use screen-like fabric to soften glare while preserving some view outside. A tighter weave generally reduces the view through the fabric; a more open weave lets you see more.

Consider: the balance of glare control and view, plus privacy after dark. Solar fabric can be see-through at night when the room is lit.

More about solar shades

Also called layered shades

Zebra shades

Alternating sheer and more opaque fabric bands move past one another to change the light and view through the shade. The horizontal pattern gives the window a distinct, layered appearance.

Compare: band alignment, fabric opacity, and the view into the room with the shade in different positions.

More about zebra shades

Also called honeycomb shades

Cellular shades

Honeycomb-shaped pockets hold air within the fabric, adding an insulating layer at the window. The pleated face offers a soft, consistent look as the shade raises and lowers.

Compare: available fabrics, light-filtering or room-darkening options, and controls that suit the height and reach of your window.

More about cellular shades

Natural texture

Woven wood shades

Woven materials bring texture and natural variation to a room. The openness of the weave affects how much light comes through and how much you can see through the shade.

Compare: the weave in daylight and with room lights on. Discuss available lining options when privacy or a darker room is a priority.

Soft light
Light-filtering fabrics let daylight through while softening it. Privacy depends on the fabric, so compare samples instead of relying on the color alone.
A darker room
Room-darkening and blackout fabrics limit light through the material. Light can still enter around the edges; mounting and fit matter as well as fabric choice.
Privacy after dark
A shade that feels private in daylight can look different with the lights on inside. This is especially important with solar fabrics, sheer bands, and open weaves.

Shade questions

Window shade questions, answered.

Need help with a particular room? Call (813) 317-1077.

What kinds of shades do you offer?

We offer roller, solar, zebra (layered), cellular (honeycomb), and woven wood shades. We’ll help you compare the styles, fabrics, and available operating systems at your consultation.

Are cellular and honeycomb shades different?

These names refer to the same shade category. The fabric forms pockets with a honeycomb shape when viewed from the side. Available fabrics and configurations vary by product.

Are zebra shades also called layered shades?

Yes. Both names describe shades with alternating sheer and more opaque horizontal bands. Moving the layers changes their alignment and the amount of light and view through the fabric.

Do solar shades provide nighttime privacy?

Don’t rely on solar screen fabric alone for nighttime privacy. People may be able to see inside when the room is lit. If privacy after dark is important, we’ll help you compare a more private fabric or an additional treatment.

Can my shades be motorized?

Rechargeable motorization is available for compatible shades. Your shade style and dimensions determine the motor and control choices. Explore rechargeable motorized shades and what to consider before ordering.

Will blackout fabric make the room completely dark?

Blackout describes the fabric’s light-blocking properties. Gaps at the sides, top, or bottom of an installed shade can still admit light. We’ll discuss fabric, mounting, and coverage based on how dark you want the room to be.

Are consultations and measurements free?

Yes. We offer free in-home consultations and measurements throughout Pinellas and Hillsborough counties. You can also visit 1876 Lake Ave SE, Unit F, Largo, FL 33771 by appointment.

Who measures and installs my shades?

Our in-house team handles your measurements and installation. We don’t use subcontractors. We’ll also confirm the expected order and installation timing for the products you choose.
