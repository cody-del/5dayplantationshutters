Start with the fabric

How much light do you want to keep?

A roller shade uses one panel of fabric that winds around a tube above the window. Light-filtering fabric softens daylight; more opaque fabric limits it. For an outward view through the lowered shade, compare solar screen fabrics.

For softer daylight

Light-filtering fabrics

Let some daylight through while taking the edge off bright light. Compare the fabric’s weave and opacity with the way you use the room.

Consider for
Living spaces where you want light while the shade is lowered.
Check before choosing
How much can be seen through the sample. Light filtering does not mean every fabric provides the same privacy.

For a darker room

Room-darkening & blackout fabrics

Choose a more opaque fabric when limiting light is the priority. Bedrooms and rooms with screens are good places to discuss how dark you want the space to feel.

Consider for
Sleeping, reducing screen glare, or limiting daylight at particular times.
Check before choosing
The mounting position and light around the edges. Blackout fabric alone does not seal the entire window.

Want to soften glare while keeping a view outside? Compare solar shades too. Solar screen fabric has different privacy considerations, especially after dark.

The details at your window

Inside or outside mount?
Compare the coverage.

Your window depth, trim, and handles affect where a shade can sit. We measure the opening and look at those details before recommending the mounting position.

For a bedroom, we’ll also discuss light around the fabric. For a frequently opened window, we’ll check clearance and how much space the roll needs above the glass.

Inside the window frame
An inside mount keeps the shade within the opening and leaves surrounding trim visible. It needs enough depth and clearance; small gaps beside the fabric can admit light.
Outside the window frame
An outside mount extends beyond the opening. Added overlap can reduce edge light, provided there is suitable mounting space and clearance for the shade to move.

What is the difference between roller and solar shades?

Both use fabric that rolls up at the top of the window. Roller fabrics offer different levels of light filtering or darkening. Solar screen fabrics are selected for glare control and some view through the shade; they should not be relied on alone for nighttime privacy.

Will blackout roller shades make my bedroom completely dark?

Blackout fabric blocks light through the material, but light can still enter around the installed shade. We’ll discuss mounting, overlap, and your room-darkening needs before recommending a configuration.

Can roller shades be motorized?

Motorized options are available, subject to the product and window size. Our motors use rechargeable batteries. We’ll confirm the motor and compatible controls. App and smart-home features depend on the selected system.

Can roller shades be used on sliding glass doors?

They can suit some openings, but access matters: the shade must be raised clear before you pass through. We’ll check the dimensions, mounting space, handles, and door use. Vertical blinds are another option to compare when you prefer a treatment that draws to the side.

Should I mount the shade inside or outside the frame?

That depends on the recess depth, obstructions, trim, and desired coverage. Inside mounting leaves more trim visible; outside mounting can add overlap. Our team measures and checks clearance before recommending either approach.
