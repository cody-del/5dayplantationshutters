The wood look, in horizontal slats

Let in daylight without lifting the blind.

Faux wood blinds use manufactured slats with a wood look. The horizontal slats tilt while the blind stays lowered, so you can change the light and view through your window throughout the day.

An everyday adjustment

Tilt the slats

Angle the slats to let more light through or close them for more privacy. How much you can see through the blind depends on the slat angle and where you’re looking from.

Think about
Street-facing windows, sunlight across a desk, and the times you use each room.
Keep in mind
Closing the slats reduces light, but small gaps between slats and around the edges can still admit it.

For a more open view

Raise the blind

Lift the slats together when you want less of the window covered. They gather beneath the headrail at the top, so the raised blind still needs space.

Think about
How often you open the window and whether furniture makes the controls difficult to reach.
Keep in mind
Opening width, blind weight, and the space occupied by the raised slats matter when choosing the operating system.

Inside the window frame
An inside mount keeps the blind within the opening and leaves surrounding trim visible. Recess depth and window hardware determine whether the slats can turn freely.
Outside the window frame
An outside mount extends beyond the opening. We check the mounting surface, overlap, and clearance so the blind can operate without catching on trim or handles.
Space above the glass
A raised blind gathers at the top. We account for that stack when discussing your view, window access, and mounting position.

Are horizontal blinds and Venetian blinds the same thing?

Venetian blinds is a common name for blinds with horizontal slats that tilt for light control. The horizontal blinds we sell are faux wood. You can also compare vertical blinds for wider openings and sliding glass doors.

Are faux wood blinds made from natural wood?

Faux wood blinds use manufactured materials to create a wood look. Natural wood blinds use wood slats. If you want a natural wood window treatment from our range, explore hardwood plantation shutters.

What slat sizes and finishes can I choose?

We’ll show you the current sizes and finishes available for the selected product. Your window dimensions, mounting depth, and desired view help guide the choice.

Will closed blinds make the room completely dark?

Closed slats limit light, but gaps between slats and around the edges can still admit it. For stronger room darkening, compare a shade with a more opaque fabric and discuss the mounting and coverage.

Can faux wood blinds be used in a kitchen or bathroom?

Tell us about heat, steam, or splashes near the window. We’ll check the selected product’s material and care guidance before recommending it for those conditions.

What operating controls are available?

Control options depend on the blind and its dimensions. We’ll demonstrate how you tilt the slats and raise or lower the selected blind, then check the control position and your reach before ordering.

Yes. Consultations and measurements are free, and our in-house team handles installation. We serve Pinellas and Hillsborough counties without subcontractors.
